1. Introduction

Heart disease is one of the leading causes of death worldwide, and identifying patterns in patient health data can help in early detection and prevention.

This project analyzes the Heart Disease dataset (originally from the UCI Machine Learning Repository and widely used on Kaggle as the “Heart Disease UCI” dataset) using R programming. The analysis focuses on patient characteristics such as chest pain type, thalassemia test results, cholesterol levels, and heart disease diagnosis outcomes.

The project uses statistical analysis and data visualization to identify important patterns and trends related to heart disease in the dataset.

2. Objectives

The main objectives of this project are:

To analyze patient records related to heart disease.
To study cholesterol level distributions.
To identify common chest pain types among patients.
To analyze the distribution of thalassemia test results among patients.
To examine the relationship between age and maximum heart rate.
To visualize important patterns using graphs.
To interpret the results and present meaningful findings.

6. Data Cleaning

The dataset uses numeric codes for some categorical variables. These are converted into readable labels to make the analysis and charts easier to interpret (e.g. chest pain type codes 1–4 become "Typical Angina", "Atypical Angina", "Non-Anginal Pain" and "Asymptomatic"; the target variable 0/1 becomes "No Disease" / "Disease Present").

The dataset contains no missing values, so no rows needed to be removed during cleaning.

14. Correlation Analysis

cor(
  heart_data$age,
  heart_data$thalach,
  use = "complete.obs"
)
[1] -0.41

Interpretation

The correlation coefficient between age and maximum heart rate is approximately -0.41. This indicates a moderate negative relationship between the two variables. This means that as a patient's age increases, their maximum heart rate tends to decrease. However, correlation does not imply causation.

15. Findings

Based on the analysis of the Heart Disease dataset, the following findings were obtained:

1) The dataset contains 247 patient records with no missing values across any of the 14 variables.

2) Asymptomatic chest pain is the most frequently reported chest pain type among patients, followed by non-anginal pain, atypical angina and typical angina.

3) A "normal" thalassemia test result is the most common outcome among patients, followed by "reversible" and "fixed" defects.

4) 66 out of 247 patients are diagnosed with heart disease, while 181 are not.

5) The cholesterol distribution shows that most patients have moderate cholesterol levels (mean 247.39 mg/dl, median 243.0 mg/dl), with a small number of unusually high observations.

6) The correlation coefficient between age and maximum heart rate is approximately -0.41, indicating a moderate negative relationship between the two variables.

7) The visualizations provide useful insights into chest pain types, thalassemia results and cholesterol patterns among heart disease patients.
